Simplified Equivalent — Gate-Source Short
With Q1 fully-on and near-zero resistance, gate, drain and source all collapse to one node — same as removing the transistor and wiring RD straight to RS.
VX = VDD × RS/(RD+RS) = 12V × 1kΩ/1.82kΩ = 6.59V
Simplified Equivalent — Drain-Source Short
Tying drain to source removes Q1 from the circuit with no assumptions needed — RG is completely untouched by this fault and still wired normally into Q1's gate pin; that connection just isn't drawn here since Q1 itself has been removed from this simplified view.
VX = VDD × RS/(RD+RS) = 12V × 1kΩ/1.82kΩ = 6.59V
Not the same reason RG was negligible in the gate-source short: there, G merged into the same node as RS, making RG a genuine (if oversized) parallel path. Here G never merges with anything — the gate stays reverse-biased and current-free exactly like the healthy circuit, so RG's own size is irrelevant.
