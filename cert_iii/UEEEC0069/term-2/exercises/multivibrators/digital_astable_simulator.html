An astable has no stable state — and no input. As soon as it's powered it keeps switching its output HIGH, LOW, HIGH, LOW on its own, making a squarewave. That's why it's used as a clock for counters and flip-flops, to flash lights, and to make tones. Pick a frequency and watch the LED and the scope.

Why R and C set the frequency
Inside a simple RC astable the capacitor C charges through R until it reaches the upper switching level — the output flips LOW and C starts discharging through R — until it reaches the lower level, the output flips HIGH, and it starts again. It never rests: no stable state. Each half takes 0.7 × R × C (like two monostable pulses back to back), so a bigger R or C gives a longer period and a lower frequency. The faint trace is the setting before your last change.
